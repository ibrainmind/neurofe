SINGLE-NODE STUDY PLAN: NETWORK DRIVE, DISEASE MARKERS AND FITTING METHODS
===========================================================================

Status : proposed, 2026-11-22
Builds on : the first single-node virtual-TMS study (experiments E1-E8)
Report : syn_brain/reports/single_node_tms_study.pdf


1. WHY THIS PLAN
----------------

The first single-node study fitted a Jansen-Rit (JR) "student" node to a
"teacher" node with neurofe's nominal parameters, using backpropagation (Adam),
under nine virtual-TMS protocols. It found:

  * At nominal values the node is almost a linear filter. With no background
    input it rests far below the firing threshold (E-I ~ -1.9 mV against
    v0 = 6 mV), where the sigmoid slope is ~0.03 out of a possible 0.7. The
    pulse response is fixed by the stimulus gain k*A and the excitatory rate a.
    The intrinsic E-I loop (c1-c4, b) only shows as a 0.6 uV late dip, close to
    the real sensor noise (0.42 uV).

  * Two scaling symmetries make some parameters unidentifiable even in
    principle:
        (A, B) x lam   with (c1..c4, k) / lam
        vmax x lam     with (A, B) / lam and k x lam
    Both change the EEG by < 1.5 uV out of 60. A, B and vmax are conventions,
    not measurements.

  * Most parameters are sloppy. Cramer-Rao bounds for one single pulse:
    k 0.9%, a 2.5%, b 14%, c1-c4 56-250%. Fisher eigenvalues span six orders
    of magnitude.

  * Stimulus design helps, but less than brain state. Multi-amplitude,
    E+I-targeted and train protocols tighten b to 0.15% and c1 to 7.5%.
    A tonic background drive above p ~ 115 cuts the c3 bound from 129% to ~5%.

  * The optimizer is a limit of its own. neurofe's raw parameterisation
    (Adam, lr 0.05) ends with ~300x higher loss and ~10x larger errors on a and
    k than log-scaled parameters, on the same data.

Open questions addressed here:

  Q-A  How can a single node be given network-like input, so that the sigmoid
       and the E-I loop take part in the response, while keeping a clean
       teacher/student test?
  Q-B  Which disease mechanisms (e.g. neurodegeneration) can TMS-EEG detect IN
       PRINCIPLE, judged from the identifiable / non-identifiable partition of
       the model, before touching real data?
  Q-C  Which fitting methods, inside or beyond neurofe's PyTorch
       backpropagation, best recover what is identifiable and report what is
       not?


2. THEORY: THE TRANSFER FUNCTION OF ONE JR NODE
-----------------------------------------------

Around its resting point a JR node is a linear system. With the synaptic kernels

    H_a(s) = a / (s+a)^2        (excitatory)
    H_b(s) = b / (s+b)^2        (inhibitory)

injected input U and EEG source V = E - I:

                           A k H_a(s)
    V(s) = -------------------------------------------------- U(s)
            1 - A S'_P H_a(s) [ L_E H_a(s) - L_I H_b(s) ]

    L_E = A c1 c2 S'_E        excitatory loop gain
    L_I = B c3 c4 S'_I        inhibitory loop gain
    S'_P, S'_E, S'_I          sigmoid slopes at the resting point

The EEG constrains only the coefficients of V(s): input gain A*k, time
constants a and b, and loop gains L_E and L_I (weighted by S'_P).
Consequences:

  1. c1 and c2 enter only as a product, as do c3 and c4. Their ratios are
     invisible in the linear regime; only the sigmoid's nonlinearity separates
     them, and only weakly.
  2. When S' is small (an inactive node) the loop terms vanish and only A*k and
     a are identifiable. This matches the measured bounds.
  3. Whatever raises S' (background drive, higher excitability) makes the loop
     identifiable. IDENTIFIABILITY DEPENDS ON BRAIN STATE, NOT ONLY ON THE
     STIMULUS.

Task T0 (section 7) checks this formula against the simulator before anything
else relies on it.


3. PART A: NETWORK-LIKE INPUT TO A SINGLE NODE (Q-A)
----------------------------------------------------

Aim
  Move the node's operating point onto the steep part of the sigmoid, as input
  from the rest of the brain would. Then measure how identifiability and
  generalization change, keeping teacher and student exactly matched
  (rung R0).

Input families

  ID  Input                              Imitates                       Known to student?
  --  ---------------------------------  -----------------------------  ---------------------------
  A1  Tonic drive p, swept 0-240         mean afferent input            known (A1a) / fitted (A1b)
                                         g * sum_j w_ij E_j
  A2  Ornstein-Uhlenbeck or 1/f noise,   ongoing resting-state          statistics known; shared
      mean mu, std sigma                 activity                       noise seed, or fit trial
                                                                        averages and spectra
  A3  Sinusoid / chirp (1-40 Hz); pulse  tACS, phase-dependent TMS      known
      at controlled phase of alpha
  A4  Delayed self-feedback (gain g,     re-entrant cortico-cortical    g, tau fitted
      delay tau): a "mirror" node        and thalamo-cortical loops
  A5  Replayed network input: the        node embedded in the real      known (open loop)
      afferent signal
      sum_j w_ij E_j(t - d_ij) a parcel
      receives in the 200-node teacher,
      injected as known drive
  A6  Population targeting: pulse into   TMS recruiting different       mixing fraction known (A6a)
      E, I, P, or mixtures               cell types                     / fitted (A6b)

How much drive is needed (fixed point of the nominal node)

  p        resting E-I     pyramidal sigmoid slope   state
  -------  --------------  ------------------------  ----------------------------------
  0        -1.9 mV         0.03                      rest, near-linear
  100      1.6 mV          0.20                      rest
  110      2.1 mV          0.25                      rest
  115-120  6.0 mV (= v0)   0.70 (max)                jump to upper branch, damped alpha
  150-220  6.3-6.7 mV      0.67-0.70                 active
  320      7.4 mV          0.61                      active

  Note: neurofe itself has no mean input. Its coupling is a Laplacian,
  g * sum_j w_ij (E_j(t - d_ij) - E_i), which is zero when all nodes rest at
  the same state, and its noise has zero mean. So every neurofe node rests in
  the inactive state, which probably explains the missing late TEP activity
  (README Finding 1). Candidate changes to neurofe, tested here first:
    - a mean input parameter p (global or per node) added to rE;
    - non-diffusive coupling g * sum_j w_ij S(E_j(t - d_ij)) (positive
      firing rates give a mean drive that grows with g and node degree);
    - noise with non-zero mean;
    - lower v0 (excitability) instead of more input.

Data types
  - evoked response (TEP), as now
  - pre-stimulus power spectrum (A2, A5): informative about b and loop gains
  - frequency response H(jw) (A3)

Measurements, for every input family
  1. Cramer-Rao bounds for the 7 fitted parameters and for the identifiable
     combinations (A*k, a, b, L_E, L_I), at nominal values and at 8 random
     teachers.
  2. Backprop recovery from 10 random starts (+-50%), noise-free and at
     0.42 uV sensor noise.
  3. Generalization to held-out stimuli and to a held-out late window
     (the E8 design), scored on both full and late windows.
  4. Operating-point checks: sigmoid slopes, distance from the p ~ 115 jump to
     the active state, and no sustained oscillation in teacher or student.

Decision
  The input family that brings the c3*c4 and c1*c2 bounds below 20% at
  realistic noise, without sustained oscillation, becomes the default
  single-node teacher for Phase 2. If none does, the late TEP cannot constrain
  the loop parameters even in the best case, and that is reported as a limit
  of the method.


4. PART B: HEALTHY VS DISEASED NODES, ON PAPER FIRST (Q-B)
----------------------------------------------------------

Aim
  Decide, from the model alone, which disease mechanisms TMS-EEG can detect,
  at what effect size, and under which stimulation and brain state. Only
  mechanisms that pass this test are taken to real data.

Parameter-to-biology map

  Parameter      Biology                    Disease link                   Identifiability (current)
  -------------  -------------------------  -----------------------------  ---------------------------
  a (1/tau_e)    AMPA/NMDA kinetics,        slowing in Alzheimer's         STIFF: identifiable from a
                 membrane time constant     disease (AD), delirium         single pulse
  b (1/tau_i)    GABA-A IPSP decay          GABAergic dysfunction,         CONDITIONAL: needs E+I
                                            benzodiazepine effects         targeting or engaged loop
  L_I ~ B*c3*c4  inhibitory loop strength   interneuron (PV) loss: AD,     CONDITIONAL: only as a
                                            epilepsy, schizophrenia        product, only when the loop
                                                                           is engaged
  L_E ~ A*c1*c2  recurrent excitation       synapse loss, the best         CONDITIONAL: as above
                                            correlate of cognitive
                                            decline in AD
  c1/c2, c3/c4   which synapse in the loop  lesion location in circuit     SLOPPY: weak, through
                 is affected                                               nonlinearity only
  A vs c's,      synapse strength vs        receptor dysfunction vs        GAUGE: never identifiable
  B vs c4        synapse number             synapse loss                   from EEG
  v0, r, vmax    excitability, threshold    hyperexcitability,             GAUGE / SLOPPY: v0 trades
                 spread, max firing rate    channelopathies                off with background drive
  A*k            input gain, incl. coil-    ATROPHY increases scalp-to-    STIFF but CONFOUNDED with
                 to-cortex distance         cortex distance, lowering k    EPSP strength A

Experiments (disease teacher changed from the nominal or the Part A teacher)

  B1  c1*c2 x 0.7        excitatory synapse loss
  B2  c3*c4 x 0.7        interneuron loss
  B3  b x 0.8            slowed GABA-A inhibition
  B4  a x 0.8            slowed excitation
  B5  v0 - 0.5 mV        hyperexcitability
  B6  k x 0.8            atrophy (confound control)
  B7  B1 + B6            synapse loss with atrophy

For each, at the resting state and at the best Part A state:
  1. Detectability: Fisher distance between healthy and diseased responses,
     d' = sqrt(dtheta' F dtheta), and the smallest change detectable at
     0.42 uV noise for a given trial count.
  2. Attribution: fitted separately to healthy and diseased teachers, does the
     student move the RIGHT combination? (In B6, does it blame k, or wrongly
     blame A*c1*c2?)
  3. State-dependent bias: fit "patients" and "controls" at different
     operating points (hypoactive diseased node vs normal control) and measure
     the spurious group difference created only by unequal identifiability.

Deliverable
  A table of mechanism x protocol x brain state with three entries each:
  detectable (d' > 2 at realistic noise), correctly attributed, free of state
  bias. This is the list of model-based biomarkers worth testing on real data.
  Each comes with the protocol and auxiliary data it needs (e.g. E-field
  modelling to fix k).

Benchmark (same teachers, protocols and noise for every method)
  - recovery of the identifiable combinations
  - calibration of reported uncertainty (does the 90% interval contain the
    truth ~90% of the time?)
  - generalization to held-out stimuli and late windows
  - wall-clock cost

Expected outcome (stated now so it can be falsified)
  - C2, C4 and C9 recover the identifiable combinations as well as or better
    than C1, in 10-100x fewer iterations.
  - No method recovers the individual c's at rest.
  - C5-C7 show the c's as prior-dominated.
  If a method recovers individual c's at rest, that contradicts section 2 and
  must be explained before going further.


6. PASS CRITERIA (TO AGREE BEFORE RUNNING)
------------------------------------------

  Part A input family : c1*c2 and c3*c4 bounds < 20% at 0.42 uV noise; no
                        sustained oscillation; backprop median error < 2x the
                        bound
  Part B marker       : d' > 2 for a 20-30% change at realistic trial counts;
                        correct attribution in >= 8 of 10 starts; state-bias
                        effect < half the true effect
  Part C method       : 90% interval coverage between 80% and 98%;
                        identifiable combinations within 2x their bounds


7. TASKS AND SCHEDULE
---------------------

All steps use the batched single-node simulator (core/node.py). One fit of
300 iterations for ~100 models takes ~20 min on 4 CPU cores.

  Week 1  T0: confirm the analytic H(s) reproduces the simulator's small-signal
          response; add A1b, A2, A3, A6 to core/node.py; Fisher sweeps.
          Gate: section 2 formula matches the simulator to < 1%.
  Week 2  A4, A5 (record afferent input in the 200-node teacher); backprop
          recovery and generalization for A1-A6.
          Gate: default teacher state chosen.
  Week 3  B1-B7 detectability and attribution, at rest and in the default
          state.
          Gate: biomarker table.
  Week 4  C2, C3, C4, C9 (deterministic methods).
          Gate: method choice for point estimates.
  Week 5  C5, C6, C8 (posteriors, noisy trials); C10 for the top markers.
          Gate: method for uncertainty; recommended protocols.
  Week 6  Write-up; move reusable parts into neurofe/validation/; feed choices
          into Phase 2 of the main study.


8. RISKS
--------

  Background drive pushes the teacher into sustained oscillation (phase-
  dependent responses, rough loss surface)
      -> keep p below the jump in A1, or fit phase-aligned averages; monitor
         pre-stimulus variance.
  Results depend on the nominal point
      -> repeat key results at 8 random teachers (+-30%), as in the current
         study.
  One-node conclusions don't carry over to the network
      -> A5 (replayed network input) is the bridge; Phase 2 re-tests the
         chosen markers in the network.
  Biology mapping too coarse (JR lumps cell types)
      -> report markers as model quantities with a stated biological reading,
         never as direct measurements.
