BACKGROUND DRIVE AND THE CONNECTION BETWEEN JR NODES  (open item, to address later)
=================================================================================

Date    : 2026-11-22
Source  : single-node virtual-TMS study (reports/single_node_tms_study.pdf,
          section 3.2) and docs/single_node_study_plan.txt, Part A
Context : came up in a discussion of how JR nodes are connected in neurofe.
          Answer given: the connection is approximately linear. The findings
          below tie directly to that.


1. HOW TO ADD BACKGROUND INPUT SO THE NODE REACHES FIRING THRESHOLD
-------------------------------------------------------------------

The pyramidal cells fire when their membrane potential E-I approaches
v0 = 6 mV. With no background input the nominal node rests at E-I = -1.9 mV.

A constant drive p into the excitatory input raises E by about
(A/a)*p = 0.0325 mV per unit of p. Recurrent excitation amplifies that, until
the node jumps across threshold (fixed point of the nominal node):

  Drive p   Resting E-I     Sigmoid slope (max 0.70)   State
  -------   -------------   ------------------------   -----------------------------------
  0         -1.9 mV         0.03                       quiet, behaves like a linear filter
  100       1.6 mV          0.20                       quiet
  110       2.1 mV          0.25                       quiet (this is where E7 ran)
  115-120   6.0 mV = v0     0.70                       jumps to the active state; rings at
                                                       ~10 Hz after a pulse
  150-220   6.3-6.7 mV      0.67-0.70                  active

In the single-node code this is just Stim(..., tonic=p) in core/node.py.
Jansen and Rit's original p was 120-320 /s, physically the mean firing rate
arriving from other areas and the thalamus.


2. NEUROFE HAS NO SUCH INPUT
----------------------------

neurofe's long-range coupling (neurofe/models/jansen_rit.py) is a Laplacian:

    g * sum_j w_ij * ( E_j(t - d_ij) - E_i )

It is zero whenever all nodes rest at the same state, and the noise input has
zero mean. So every node in a neurofe network sits in the quiet, near-linear
state. That most likely explains why synthetic responses lacked late activity
(Finding 1 in syn_brain/README.md).


3. OPTIONS (also in the study plan and the report's recommendations)
--------------------------------------------------------------------

  a. Add a mean-input parameter p, global or per node, to the excitatory
     drive rE.
  b. Switch to non-diffusive coupling  g * sum_j w_ij * S(E_j(t - d_ij)):
     neighbours send firing rates, which are positive, so each node receives
     a net drive that grows with g and its degree.
  c. Use noise with a non-zero mean.
  d. Lower v0 (more excitable cells), which moves the threshold toward the
     resting point instead.


4. NEXT STEP (addressed below)
-------------------------------

The most useful next run is fitting at p >= 120. E7 used p = 110, just below
the jump, and showed no gain; the Cramer-Rao bounds predict a large one above
it (single-pulse bound on c3: 129% at rest -> ~5% at p = 120).


5. RESULT: E9/E10, BACKPROP RECOVERY AT p = 120 AND p = 150  (2026-11-22)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

Added tonic drive p = 120 (just past the jump) and p = 150 (settled on the
active branch) alongside the existing p = 110, as new Stim entries in
core/node.py (FIRING_PS), and two new experiments in single_node_tms.py:
  E9   same design as E7 (noise-free), at p = 120 and p = 150
  E10  same, at the real sensor noise level (0.42 uV)
10 random starts, SP1000 and DOSE protocols, 300 Adam iterations, log-scaled
parameters -- same recipe as every other experiment in this study.

Analytic CRB confirms the prediction almost exactly: at p = 120 the SP1000
bound on c3 drops from 129% (rest) to 4.9%, c4 from 252% to 16%, b from 14%
to 8%. No change at p = 150 (4.1%, 15%, 7%) -- the loop is identifiable in
principle as soon as the node is pushed past the jump.

Backprop recovery does NOT uniformly reach that bound:

  combined-parameter error (median of 10 starts, SP1000 / DOSE, noise-free E9):
    p = 120:  c3*c4  52-73%   c1*c2  56-75%   (far above the <20% target,
              and above even the REST-state CRB for c1*c2 -- the optimizer is
              doing worse than chance near the threshold crossing)
    p = 150:  c3*c4  14-16%   c1*c2  20-27%   (c3*c4 meets the <20% pass
              criterion; c1*c2 is close but still does not clear it)

  Sensor noise (E10, 0.42 uV) changes these numbers by only a few percent in
  either direction -- optimization difficulty, not measurement noise, is the
  binding constraint at p = 120.

  No sustained oscillation at either p: pre-stimulus std stays at 0.008-0.017
  uV (p=120/150), far under the 0.42 uV noise floor; the predicted ~10 Hz
  ringing after the pulse is damped, as expected, not self-sustaining.

Interpretation: p = 120 sits exactly at the bifurcation (table in section 1),
where the loss surface is apparently rough enough that 300 Adam iterations
from +-50% random starts don't find the optimum the Fisher information says
exists -- a genuine "optimizer can't reach it" failure, not a non-identifiability.
p = 150, solidly on the active branch and away from the jump, is the better
operating point: it clears the inhibitory-loop (c3*c4) pass criterion and gets
close on the excitatory loop (c1*c2), which stays the harder of the two to
recover even in the best state tested here.

Practical takeaway for Part A of the study plan: when adding background drive
(tonic p, or any other input family), land cleanly on the active branch
(p ~ 150, not p ~ 115-120) -- the threshold crossing itself is a bad place to
sit, both for a real brain state and for fitting it.

Not yet done: more starts / iterations or a better optimizer (C2-C4 in the
study plan) to check whether p = 120's gap closes with more optimization
effort, which would confirm it is optimizer-limited rather than something
else going wrong near the bifurcation; and the other input families (A2-A6)
that might reach the active branch more smoothly than a step in tonic drive.
